Opening a Restaurant in Washington, DC: Permits and Approvals

Zelta Design · 4 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

In Washington, DC, the Department of Buildings runs one permit review that pulls in every other agency: DC Health, DC Water, DDOT, the environment department, energy and green building, mechanical and plumbing, and zoning. On a new drive-thru building we designed, the first review round came back with comments from all of them in one letter, so the drawings, the health packet and the utility forms all need to be ready together.

In Washington, DC, one agency issues the building permit and every other agency reviews through it. The Department of Buildings, DOB, takes the application and the drawings, then routes them to DC Health, DC Water, the Department of Transportation (DDOT), the Department of Energy and Environment, and its own energy, mechanical, plumbing and zoning reviewers. Their comments come back together.

That makes the District simpler to file in than many states, and harder to rush. On a new drive-thru restaurant building we designed in Southeast DC, the first review round in 2024 returned comments from eight disciplines in one letter. We are licensed in the District, and this guide covers what each of those reviewers asked for and how to be ready for them.

One application, many reviewers

DOB is the front door, and its comments arrive as one combined letter. Plan to answer every discipline in the same resubmittal.

Construction drawings go to DOB through its ProjectDox system. DC Health’s plan review page says to start the building permit application first, then submit the DC Health Plan Review Guidance Packet through ProjectDox as well. The health review is part of the building permit, not a separate track.

The codes are the District’s own. Our 2024 review was under the 2017 DC Construction Codes, which add District amendments to the model codes, and DOB has announced its move to newer editions. Ask which edition applies on your filing date.

DC Health

DC Health reviews the kitchen as part of the permit. On our 2024 project its comments asked for:

  • a written response describing how each comment was addressed;
  • the menu;
  • a service sink, often called a mop sink;
  • an indirect connection at the three-compartment sink, with each well draining separately to a floor sink.

The plumbing reviewer raised the same sink detail under the DC Plumbing Code, and noted that the grease interceptor’s outlet must connect directly to the sanitary line. Draw it right the first time and two reviewers sign off at once. Our health department plan review guide covers the packet, and our grease guide covers interceptors.

Energy and green building

The District reviews energy closely, even on a small restaurant. DOB assigns a green review to projects of 10,000 square feet or more and an energy review to smaller ones; our restaurant got the energy review.

On our project, the energy reviewer asked for:

  • the District’s energy verification sheet completed, with the drawing page where each item is shown;
  • a fenestration schedule with the U-factor and solar heat gain figures for the glazing and doors;
  • roof, wall and slab insulation shown in section with R-values;
  • confirmation that cooking equipment such as ovens and hot holding cabinets meets ENERGY STAR equivalent criteria.

The project followed the prescriptive path, a checklist of minimum values, rather than a whole-building energy model, settled in a conversation with the reviewer. Settle the path early, and fill in the verification sheet before the first submission. Our energy code guide explains the paperwork.

Mechanical, zoning and the street

Three more reviewers are easy to underestimate.

  • Mechanical. The reviewer cited a District amendment to the mechanical code requiring a 20 foot clearance between exhaust outlets and air intakes, or a height of at least 5 feet above them. Lay out the roof with the District’s distances, not the model code’s. See kitchen engineering.
  • Zoning. Our site was covered by an order of the Board of Zoning Adjustment. DOB required its BZA and ZC plans modification form: either the permit plans match the approved exhibits exactly, or you submit a written summary and side-by-side comparison of the changes.
  • DDOT. Any work in the sidewalk or street needs to be dimensioned on the site plan, and DDOT issues public space permits for it through its online system.

Water, power, gas and an address

A new building needs its utilities lined up alongside the permit. DC Water asked for the site and civil drawings to be sent to its own review, with its standard forms, including the meter sizing worksheet and the backflow prevention form. Pepco and Washington Gas each took their own service applications.

A new building may also need a new street address. DOB’s Permit Operations Division assigned ours in 2024, with the letter requiring the address to be posted on the building. DOB explains the request on its new address page. Request it early, because every later application should carry the same address.

Special inspections and opening

Structural and other critical work in the District is checked by special inspections. Under DOB’s Special Inspection Policy Manual, a statement of special inspections goes in with the permit application, and the final report must be accepted before the building final inspection. On our project the architect and the structural engineer signed the acknowledgment of those requirements at the start, and the engineer’s final report closed them out.

After that come the final inspections, DC Health’s pre-opening inspection and the certificate of occupancy. See the certificate of occupancy.

A checklist before you sign the lease

  • Check whether the site is under a BZA or Zoning Commission order.
  • Gather the menu and equipment sheets for the DC Health packet.
  • Choose the energy compliance path and fill in the verification sheet.
  • Lay out the roof with the District’s exhaust clearances.
  • Start DC Water, Pepco, Washington Gas and any address request with the permit.
  • Line up the special inspections agency before filing.

Questions

Common Questions

Do I file with DC Health separately from the building permit?

Not separately in practice. DC Health says to apply for the building permit with the Department of Buildings first, then submit its Plan Review Guidance Packet through the same ProjectDox system, and its comments come back inside the building permit review.

What did DC Health ask for on a restaurant permit?

On our 2024 review it asked for a written response to each comment, the menu, a mop sink, and an indirect connection for each well of the three-compartment sink.

Does a small DC restaurant need a green building review?

DOB assigns a green review to projects of 10,000 square feet or more and an energy review to smaller ones. A typical restaurant gets the energy review, which still checks the envelope, the lighting and the kitchen equipment.

What are special inspections in DC?

Third-party inspections of work such as structure and foundations. A statement of special inspections goes in with the permit application, and DOB must accept the final report before the building final inspection.

Permits by State guide

Opening a Restaurant in Maryland: Permits and Approvals

Zelta Design · 5 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

In Maryland, a restaurant's building permit comes from the county or, inside some cities, from the city, under building codes the state requires every jurisdiction to use. The local health department must approve kitchen plans before construction, fire review sits with the state or a county fire office depending on the address, and in Prince George's County the regional planning commission checks zoning before a use and occupancy permit is issued.

In Maryland, the building permit comes from the county or, inside some incorporated cities, from the city. The kitchen goes to the local health department. Fire review sits with the state fire marshal or a county fire office, depending on the address. In Prince George’s County, a regional planning commission also checks zoning before the use and occupancy permit is issued. Which offices apply, and in what order, depends on the parcel and the scope, so additional approvals are common.

The technical rules are broadly the same across the state. The Maryland Building Performance Standards require every jurisdiction to use the same edition of the same building codes, though local governments may amend them for local conditions. Zelta Design is licensed in Maryland, and our restaurants there include our Taco Bell in Landover and our Taco Bell in Oxon Hill, both freestanding buildings, along with newer work in western Maryland.

Step 1: City or county

Start with the parcel. The county is usually the permit office, but some incorporated cities run their own.

Near Hagerstown we saw this split on two jobs with Hagerstown mailing addresses. A store outside city limits went to Washington County, while a store with two restaurant brands inside the city went to the City of Hagerstown. Confirm the jurisdiction from the county’s parcel records before preparing anything, because it decides who reviews the site plan, issues the permit and inspects.

In Prince George’s County, the county’s Department of Permitting, Inspections and Enforcement, called DPIE, reviews building, fire and health plans. The Maryland-National Capital Park and Planning Commission, the planning agency for Prince George’s and Montgomery counties, handles zoning, use and site plans. Its zoning approval comes before DPIE issues building permits.

Step 2: The site plan for a drive-thru pad

A new drive-thru on a pad site is a land use project before it is a building project. Near Hagerstown, Washington County’s planning office told us a site plan was required, prepared by a qualified professional and sent out for review by the county’s agencies. The county runs that review through its development plan review process, and it would not accept the building permit until the site plan was approved.

The site plan has to meet local rules, not only the brand’s. On that job:

  • Queuing. Check the brand’s sample layout against the county’s queue rule before drawing. Our drive-thru design guide explains how stacking rules are counted.
  • Parking. Ask the county whether this pad changes the parking review for the whole mall.
  • Accessible parking. The county wanted the accessible space inside the restaurant’s own lot, while other spaces could be across the road.
  • Approvals in a row. The landlord reviewed the site plan first, then the brand, before it went to the county.

Step 3: The building permit

For most restaurant work, the permit drawings are sealed by a Maryland-licensed architect. State law, Business Occupations and Professions 3-501, requires a licensed architect to sign, seal and date architectural documents needed for a building permit, subject to narrow exceptions in 3-103. Our guide on whether you need an architect covers the exceptions. We check every sealed sheet carries the Maryland seal before anything is filed.

Many offices take applications and revisions online. In Washington County, we resubmitted revisions through the county’s permit portal.

Some offices hold the final building approval until other agencies sign off. On the Hagerstown store inside the city, health and fire approval were needed before full building approval. Run those reviews alongside the building review, not after it. Our permit timeline guide explains how gates like these add up. Any change from the sealed drawings during construction should come back to the architect in writing before it is built.

Step 4: Health department plan review

State food rules, COMAR 10.15.03.33, require kitchen plans to be approved before a food establishment is built, remodeled or materially altered. In practice, the local health department reviews them.

  • Prince George’s County. The county health department’s plan review runs through DPIE’s health section, with electronic submittal. It does not cover federal buildings or federal property.
  • Washington County. On our Hagerstown-area jobs, kitchen plans went to the Washington County Health Department.
  • Chain prototypes. The state’s Center for Facility and Process Review reviews prototypical food service facilities. Ask the local office whether a brand prototype changes the route.

The health department also inspects before opening. Near Hagerstown, the opening list included self-closing restroom doors and a copy of the fire marshal’s approval. Our health department plan review guide covers the full packet.

Step 5: The fire marshal

Fire review is done by the Office of the State Fire Marshal or a local fire office, depending on the county. The state’s inspections page lists jurisdictions with their own offices, including Prince George’s, Montgomery, Anne Arundel, Baltimore County, Baltimore City, Frederick, Howard and Worcester. Arrangements change. Since July 1, 2025, Washington County’s permits division handles fire plan review and inspections the state used to do there. Keep the fire approval, because other agencies may ask for it.

Prince George’s County: use and occupancy

In Prince George’s County, a restaurant needs a use and occupancy permit, the county’s permission to run that use in that space, and the planning agency must clear zoning before DPIE can issue it. Older restaurants need extra care.

If a restaurant is a nonconforming use, the county requires a use and occupancy permit that identifies it as nonconforming and a certification of the use. The planning department’s certification procedure asks for evidence that the use has not stopped for more than 180 consecutive calendar days since it became nonconforming. Before you buy or remodel an older Prince George’s store, ask whether its use is nonconforming and collect opening and sales records early. Other counties set their own rules. Our guide to second-generation restaurant space explains why a space’s history matters.

Some owners hire a permit expediter, a specialist who walks applications through county offices. Our guide to who does what on a restaurant project explains how that role fits.

Inspections and opening

The office that issued the permit inspects each trade and issues the final approval, often a use and occupancy permit. The health department does its own opening inspection, and the fire office may do a final walk through. Ask each office at the start what it needs at the end, including which approved drawings must stay on site.

A checklist before you sign the lease

  • Confirm from parcel records whether the site is in a city or the county.
  • For a drive-thru pad, ask whether a site plan is required, and check the brand layout against local queuing and parking rules.
  • Start health review as soon as the drawings allow.
  • Find out which fire office covers the address.
  • For an older Prince George’s store, confirm the use is still valid and gather proof of continuous operation.
  • Keep every approval letter; the next agency may ask for it.

Questions

Common Questions

Who issues a restaurant building permit in Maryland?
Usually the county. Some incorporated cities run their own permit offices, so a site inside city limits goes to the city and one just outside goes to the county, even when both share a mailing address. Check the parcel records first.
Does a drive-thru restaurant in Maryland need a site plan?
Often, yes, on a new pad. Near Hagerstown we saw Washington County require site plan approval before it would accept the building permit, and the site plan had to meet the county's own queuing and parking rules, not just the brand's sample layout.
Can a Maryland restaurant lose its right to operate as a restaurant?
In Prince George's County it can, if the restaurant is a nonconforming use, one the current zoning would not allow if it were new. The county's planning department asks for proof the use has not stopped for more than 180 consecutive days. Other counties have their own rules.
Does the health department have to approve before construction in Maryland?
Yes. State food rules require kitchen plans to be approved by the health authority, usually the local health department, before a food establishment is built, remodeled or altered. Some building offices also hold the building approval until health and fire sign off.

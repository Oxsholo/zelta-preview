Opening a PopUp Bagels: Design, Permits and Build-Out

Zelta Design · 4 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

A PopUp Bagels shop is a small grab-and-go store focused on ordering, pickup and retail, with little seating and a bold, consistent brand look. Opening one means finding a compact space that can take the kitchen's water, power and grease needs, fitting the brand's design to it, and clearing landlord, planning, building, fire and health reviews that vary a lot from state to state.

To open a PopUp Bagels you need a compact space that can support a real kitchen, a layout that carries the brand’s look into that space, and approvals that change a great deal by location: the landlord, sometimes a planning or historic commission, the building department, the fire reviewer and the health department. The shops are small, but the review list is not.

We have designed PopUp Bagels shops since 2022, in Florida, New Jersey, New York, Connecticut, Ohio and Michigan. This guide covers design, permits and build-out. For investment figures, read Item 7 of the brand’s Franchise Disclosure Document, the estimated initial investment table required by the FTC Franchise Rule; the FTC’s guide to buying a franchise explains how to read it.

What a PopUp Bagels shop is

The format is grab-and-go. Customers order, collect and leave, and the room is planned around that. Our published shops show the pattern:

  • Winter Park, Florida. A freestanding building with a corner glass storefront. The 953-square-foot interior is given to ordering, pickup and retail rather than seating, with yellow stools outside for grab-and-go customers and a small waiting nook by the window.
  • Rockledge, Florida. An inline bay of 1,167 square feet with a ribbed black counter, white subway tile behind the kitchen pass and a chalkboard menu, with stools out front for walk-up customers.
  • Westfield, New Jersey. A freestanding building with its own parking lot, where a small seating area sits against a full-height photo collage wall.

The brand look carries across all three: black and white checkerboard floors, a photo collage wall and bold signage. Brand packages change, so confirm with the brand which version is approved for your shop before design starts. Our job is to fit that look to very different buildings.

Choosing the space

Small does not mean simple. Before signing, check:

  • Hot water. Settle the water heating early. Owners on our Florida shops have chosen tankless water heaters, which need gas or electric capacity to match and wall space where they can be serviced.
  • Grease. Local rules vary. Some counties run grease review as its own track alongside the building permit, with its own closeout, and some cities split grease interceptor and service port questions between different reviewers. Ask early who reviews grease and what they need.
  • Signage power. Plan the electrical feed for the exterior sign early, so the box lands where the sign maker needs it.
  • Service doors. Check what the lease already says about trash rooms and service doors. Landlords often fix these in the lease, and the design has to follow it.

Our lease checklist covers the building facts to settle in writing, and our guide to small-footprint restaurants covers what changes when a store is this compact.

Planning and historic review

Some PopUp Bagels sites sit in downtowns and older neighborhoods, where a planning or historic review comes before the building permit.

  • Historic districts. A certificate of appropriateness from the district’s commission is often required before the building permit can be issued. Commissions comment on storefronts and signs, and a written response letter with marked-up exhibits is the usual way to answer.
  • Planning review. Some cities review the plans before the building department does, and expect a resubmitted, sealed set with a written response to each comment before the project can move on.

See restaurants in historic buildings for how those reviews work.

Designing a compact back of house

Most of the hard decisions in a PopUp Bagels are behind the counter, where the bagel production, storage, walk-in and dish area share a small floor.

Exit paths. In a compact plan it is tempting to route a storage room’s exit through the kitchen. The International Building Code (2021 edition, section 1016.2) says egress shall not pass through kitchens, storage rooms or closets, with narrow exceptions, and state fire codes add their own versions of the rule. The fix is a layout change, so catch it in the test fit. Our fire protection guide covers the fire reviewer’s side.

Complete submissions. Some building departments want the walk-in cooler shop drawings, the fabricator’s detailed drawings, included with the permit submission rather than sent later as a deferred submittal. Ask before you file.

Paper sets. Some health departments still want a printed, scaled plan set by mail even after an online upload. Ask each agency how it wants to receive plans.

Detail-level review

PopUp Bagels drawings get reviewed down to finishes and fixtures: outlet heights that match the brand’s shelf details, and the order of work so a wall receiving a painted mural gets its base color before the muralist arrives. Expect the brand to keep a copy of the construction set submitted to the building department, so its records match what the town is reviewing.

Details like these matter because the brand experience depends on them. The photo walls, murals and signs are the store’s identity, and they need power, blocking and finishes planned before construction. See how franchise brand approvals work for the general approval sequence.

Health department review

Most jurisdictions require food service plan review before a new shop opens. The packet needs the menu, equipment schedule and food handling details from the operator as well as the drawings, so start it alongside the building set (see health department plan review). Who reviews it depends on the address. In Florida, the state’s Division of Hotels and Restaurants handles plan review for new builds, conversions and remodels of public food service establishments, while bakeries go to the state agriculture department, so a bagel shop should confirm which agency licenses it (see opening a restaurant in Florida). In many other states a county or city health department does it.

Questions

Common Questions

How big is a PopUp Bagels shop?

Small. Our Winter Park, Florida shop is 953 square feet and our Rockledge, Florida shop is 1,167 square feet. Both put the space into ordering, pickup and retail rather than seating.

Does a PopUp Bagels need a dining room?

Usually not much of one. Our Florida shops rely on stools outside for waiting customers, while our Westfield, New Jersey shop has a small seating area. Less seating can keep the occupant load, restroom count and exits simpler.

What should I watch for in a small bagel shop's back of house?

The exit path. Building codes generally do not allow the way out to pass through a kitchen or storeroom, so a storage room at the back needs its own route to an exit. Settle that in the test fit, because fixing it later means moving walls.

Can a PopUp Bagels go in a historic district?

Yes, but expect an extra review. Many historic districts require a certificate of appropriateness from a historic commission before the building permit is issued, and the commission reviews the storefront and signs.

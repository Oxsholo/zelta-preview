Opening a Restaurant in Florida: Permits and Approvals

Zelta Design · 5 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

In Florida, the building permit comes from the city building department, or from the county for unincorporated land, under the statewide Florida Building Code. The local fire official reviews fire and life safety. Food licensing is state run: most restaurants are licensed by DBPR's Division of Hotels and Restaurants, while bakeries, grocery and convenience stores fall under FDACS. Zoning, grease, signs and other approvals depend on the site and scope.

Florida splits a restaurant’s approvals between local government and the state. The building permit comes from the city building department, or from the county if the property is on unincorporated land, under the statewide Florida Building Code. The local fire official reviews fire and life safety. The food license comes from a state agency, not a local health department, and which agency depends on what you sell. Zoning, grease, signs and site work add other approvals depending on the property and the scope.

We are licensed in Florida, and our Florida work includes the PopUp Bagels in Winter Park, a freestanding stucco building, and the PopUp Bagels in Rockledge, an inline bay in a shopping center. Here is how the approvals fit together.

Step 1: City, county or someone else

Start by finding out which local government has the parcel. Mailing addresses do not settle this, because many unincorporated communities use a nearby city’s name.

  • Inside a city. Many Florida cities run their own building department and zoning. Some contract building review to the county or a private firm, so ask who actually reviews and inspects.
  • Unincorporated land. The county building department and county zoning review the project.
  • Fire. The Florida Fire Prevention Code is enforced by the local fire official in each county, city or special fire district, which is not always the same government that issues the building permit.

Check the county property appraiser’s record for the parcel (the Florida Department of Revenue links every county’s appraiser), then confirm with the building department before you file.

The same local government usually runs zoning. In an existing shopping center unit, that is often a staff check of use and parking. A freestanding building, a drive-thru or new signs can bring site plan review and the sign code into play.

Step 2: The building permit

The building permit is reviewed against the Florida Building Code, which the state updates every three years. The 8th Edition (2023) is in force as of this writing, and a 9th Edition (2026) is in preparation, so confirm which edition applies on your filing date.

For most restaurant work, the drawings must be signed and sealed by an architect licensed in Florida. Section 481.229 of the Florida Statutes lists exemptions, including buildings costing less than $25,000, except schools, auditoriums and other buildings intended for public use. Our architect requirement guide covers when one applies.

Florida departments now take most filings through online portals, and each portal has its own rules. Before uploading, check with your building department, or your expediter, on:

  • One file or many. Some offices want each sheet as its own PDF. Others want the whole set as one file.
  • Signing. Whether a digital signature is needed on every sheet, and whether files must be flattened so no drawing layers remain.
  • File names. Whether file names have to match the sheet numbers in the title blocks.
  • Separate trade permits. Whether the kitchen hood, signs or fire alarm go in as their own applications. In Pasco County, for example, the hood was filed under a separate application from the building permit.
  • The address. Use one address, in one format, on the application, every title block and every form.

Our construction documents guide explains what goes in the set.

Wind design and product approvals

Florida designs for hurricanes, and it controls which exterior products may be used. Settle these products early, with their approvals in hand.

Under Section 553.842 of the Florida Statutes, panel walls, exterior doors, roofing, skylights, windows, shutters, impact protective systems and certain structural components need a product approval, either statewide or from the local building department. You can look up a statewide approval on the Florida Building Commission’s product approval search. Each approval has limits, such as the wind pressure it was tested for, and the product must be used within them at your site.

Rooftop equipment is handled differently. The Florida Building Code, Mechanical (Section 301.15) requires exposed equipment and its supports to be designed and installed to resist wind. The engineer shows the anchorage on the drawings.

Miami-Dade and Broward counties form the code’s High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, with stricter testing and review. A contractor’s late substitution of a storefront, door or rooftop unit can send the drawings back through review, so lock in the products before filing.

Fire review

The local fire official reviews the exits, fire alarm, sprinklers and the kitchen hood’s fire suppression system. Florida’s life safety code generally does not let the path to an exit run through a kitchen or storeroom. Our fire protection guide covers how that shapes a small back of house.

Step 3: State food licensing

Florida licenses restaurants at the state level, and the first job is picking the right agency. Per DBPR’s own jurisdiction page:

  • The Division of Hotels and Restaurants, part of the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR), licenses freestanding restaurants, fast food and takeout, mobile food units and bars that serve food. Its plan review is required before licensing when a place is newly built, converted from another use, remodeled, or reopened after being closed at least 18 months.
  • The Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS) licenses bakeries, grocery stores and convenience stores. Its retail food permit page describes a voluntary plan review against its minimum construction standards.

A burger or chicken restaurant normally goes to DBPR. A bakery, a coffee and pastry shop or a concept with substantial retail food sales can land on either side, so we confirm the agency with the owner before preparing the packet. Our health department plan review guide covers what the packet contains.

Grease and utilities

Grease rules in Florida usually come from the local utility or county, not the state food agency. In Orange County, for example, the grease program ran as its own approval, closed separately from the building permit. Ask the utility early what it requires, because the answer can change the interceptor’s size and location.

Accessibility

Florida has its own accessibility code, the Florida Building Code, Accessibility, based on the federal standards with state additions. Reviewers apply it to entrances, counters, restrooms and routes through the dining room. Our accessibility guide covers the details.

Expediters and private providers

Many Florida owners hire a permit expediter to file and track the building permit, and several of our Florida projects have been filed that way. An expediter speeds up the paperwork, not the review. Ask early what they will need from the owner and architect, such as a short description of each revision and the construction value, and send it with the drawings. Florida also lets owners hire a private provider for plan review and inspections; our guide to speeding up permits explains how that works.

Inspections and opening

The building department inspects each trade and issues the certificate of occupancy after final inspections, usually with a fire inspection alongside. The state food agency inspects before it issues the license. Schedule both early, because a restaurant cannot open on a building approval alone. Our certificate of occupancy guide covers the closeout.

Questions

Common Questions

Who issues a restaurant building permit in Florida?

Inside a city, usually the city's building department, although some cities contract the work to the county or a private firm. On unincorporated land, the county. All of them enforce the same statewide Florida Building Code. Confirm the jurisdiction from county parcel records, not the mailing address.

Which state agency licenses a restaurant in Florida?

DBPR's Division of Hotels and Restaurants licenses freestanding restaurants, fast food, takeout and bars that serve food, and reviews plans for new, converted and remodeled ones. FDACS licenses bakeries, grocery stores and convenience stores. If your concept sits near that line, ask both agencies before you file.

Do Florida restaurants need hurricane-rated doors and storefronts?

Exterior doors, windows, panel walls, skylights, shutters, roofing and impact protection need a Florida product approval, statewide or local, and must be used within its limits. Rooftop equipment has to be anchored for wind. Miami-Dade and Broward counties apply the code's stricter High-Velocity Hurricane Zone rules.

Should I use a permit expediter in Florida?

Many Florida owners do, and several of our Florida projects were filed by one. An expediter speeds up filing and tracking, not the review itself, and only works well when the owner and architect hand over a complete package with the drawings.

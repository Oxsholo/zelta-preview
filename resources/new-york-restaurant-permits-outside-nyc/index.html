Permits by State guide

Opening a Restaurant in New York Outside NYC: Permits

Zelta Design · 6 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

Outside New York City, a restaurant's building permit usually comes from the city, town or village where the property sits, under the statewide Uniform Fire Prevention and Building Code, though a few local governments have handed enforcement to their county or the state. The same local government runs zoning, which can mean a special use permit or site plan approval, and the county or state health office issues the food service permit. The first job is working out which local government you are actually in.

Outside the five boroughs, the key distinction is between local government and state code. Every city, town and incorporated village is required by default to enforce the New York State Uniform Fire Prevention and Building Code, the building code for everywhere in the state except New York City. The Department of State’s guide for local officials explains the exception: a local government can opt out, which passes enforcement to its county, and if the county also opts out, to the Department of State. The same local government writes the zoning, and a separate health agency issues the food service permit. Which further approvals you need depends on the property and the scope.

So the first question is simple to ask and easy to get wrong: which government are you in? After that, the work follows a pattern. Find out what the local boards need to see, file the building permit with drawings sealed by a New York licensed architect, and run the health review in parallel. We are licensed in New York and have designed restaurants from Long Island and Westchester to the Capital Region, Central New York and the Niagara Frontier. For the five boroughs, see our New York City permit guide.

Step 1: Work out which government you are in

New York’s local government map is layered. Every part of the state outside the cities belongs to a town. Inside a town, some communities are incorporated villages with their own building department, zoning code and boards. Others are hamlets, communities with a name and a post office but no government of their own, so the town handles everything.

This matters most on Long Island and in Westchester and Rockland, where villages and hamlets sit side by side. A mailing address does not tell you which one you are in. We confirm the reviewing government from the property records before we price the approvals, and we ask the building department whether it enforces the code itself.

Step 2: Zoning and the boards

Each local government writes its own zoning, and a board can be involved for two different reasons.

Because of the use. Many codes allow restaurants or drive-thrus only with a special use permit. Under Town Law section 274-b, a special use permit authorizes a use the zoning allows subject to conditions, granted by the planning board or another board the town designates. Village and city law has similar provisions.

Because of the work. Changes to the site or the outside of the building bring in other reviews:

  • The planning board reviews site plans, under Town Law section 274-a in towns: parking, circulation, drive-thru lanes, landscaping and often signs.
  • The zoning board of appeals hears variances when the project does not meet the zoning.
  • An architectural review board, where one exists, reviews the look of the building and its signs.

If the use is permitted as of right and the work stays inside, many places issue the building permit with no hearing. The trigger for a board can be small, though. On a drive-thru remodel in a Capital Region town, the planning board had to approve the new menu board, the restriping of the parking lot and the signs, while the interior work, a new sink, counter and paint, did not need a building permit at all. In a Westchester city, a small addition where an old walk-in cooler had stood went to the planning board.

That is why our intake checklist asks the building department two questions in writing before design starts: is the use permitted as of right, and does anything in the scope, including additions, menu boards and signs, need a board? Brand reimage programs often change exterior elements, so we ask again when the brand’s package arrives. Our site feasibility guide covers what to check before you sign.

Step 3: The building permit

The building permit is filed with the local building department, and the drawings must be sealed by a New York licensed architect or engineer. Smaller departments often have one or two people doing plan review, inspections and code enforcement, so pace and format vary widely.

What to settle with the department before filing:

  • Review time. It varies by department. Ask what to expect before you set an opening date.
  • Level of detail. Some reviewers want more than others. One Westchester city asked us for large scale storefront details at the head, jamb and sill, showing how the frame attached to the existing wall and structure above.
  • How they accept drawings. Ask whether the department takes electronic files, through which portal or address and at what size, or wants paper sets. Small departments differ, and the answer decides how the set is packaged.

Step 4: Fire marshals

On Long Island especially, fire code review and inspections often sit with a town or county fire marshal rather than the building department. On a Suffolk County job, the sprinkler and fire alarm work ran on a separate fire permit, and whoever held that permit had to schedule the rough fire prevention inspection, because it could not be booked under the building permit. Decide which contractor holds each permit before work starts. Our kitchen engineering guide covers the hood and suppression systems those inspectors check.

Step 5: Health review

The state Department of Health says food service permits are issued by the local health department responsible for the area. That is a county health department in many counties and a state district office elsewhere. Many review the kitchen plans before construction. Suffolk County, for example, requires applications and floor plans to be approved before construction starts when a site has not held a county permit in the last two years or when structural changes are made.

Suffolk adds a wastewater question. Many sites there are not on public sewer, and the county’s Office of Wastewater Management says new or modified commercial buildings may need its approval under Article 6 of the county sanitary code. Confirm that early for a freestanding building. Our health department plan review guide covers what goes in the packet.

Malls, mixed-use buildings and drive-thrus

The building type shapes the approvals as much as the town does. A few of our completed restaurants outside the city show the range:

  • Wendy’s in the Green Acres Mall food court in Valley Stream, an 811 square foot inline unit.
  • A freestanding 24-hour Dunkin’ drive-thru in Lindenhurst, built in the shape of a shingled cottage.
  • A two-story corner Dunkin’ in Nyack with a separate drive-thru side entrance.
  • A Jimmy John’s in a new mixed-use building in Albany.
  • A Dunkin’ in Guilderland that added customer seating and a drive-thru.

Each one needed a different mix of landlord, board, building, fire and health approvals.

A checklist before you sign the lease

  • Confirm whether the property is in a city, a town or an incorporated village, and who enforces the building code there.
  • Ask whether a restaurant or drive-thru is a permitted use or needs a special use permit.
  • Ask whether the work will need site plan, zoning board or architectural review.
  • Find out who does fire code review and who will hold the fire permit.
  • Confirm the health agency and whether it reviews plans before construction.
  • For a freestanding site, ask whether it is on public sewer.

Questions

Common Questions

Who issues a restaurant building permit on Long Island or in Westchester?
Usually the local government where the property sits: a city, a town or an incorporated village. If the address is a hamlet, an unincorporated community, the town reviews it. A local government can opt out of enforcing the state code, in which case the county or the New York Department of State takes over.
Does a restaurant outside NYC need planning board approval?
It depends on the local zoning. A board can be involved because of the use itself, when the code lists restaurants or drive-thrus as needing a special use permit, or because of the work, when it changes the site, the footprint or the exterior. Some interior-only jobs in a permitted use need no board at all.
Is the New York City building code used outside the five boroughs?
No. New York City kept its own construction codes and Department of Buildings. The rest of the state uses the New York State Uniform Fire Prevention and Building Code, enforced locally.
Who does health review for a restaurant outside New York City?
The local health department responsible for the area, which is a county health department in many counties, including Nassau, Suffolk and Westchester, and a regional office of the state Department of Health elsewhere.

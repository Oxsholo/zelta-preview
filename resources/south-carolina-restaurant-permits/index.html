Permits by State guide

Opening a Restaurant in South Carolina: Permits and Approvals

Zelta Design · 7 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

In South Carolina, the building permit comes from the city if the site is inside city limits and from the county if not, under building codes adopted statewide. The food permit comes from the South Carolina Department of Agriculture, which took over retail food safety in 2024. The local business license usually follows the certificate of occupancy, and other approvals depend on the site and scope.

In South Carolina, a restaurant answers to two separate systems. The building, its trades and the certificate of occupancy go through the local building department: the city’s if the site is inside city limits, the county’s if it is not. The food permit comes from the state, through the South Carolina Department of Agriculture. Fire safety is reviewed locally, so ask the building department who reviews fire plans for your address. Zoning, driveway, landlord and sewer approvals are added depending on the site and the scope of work.

We are licensed in South Carolina and design restaurants across the state, including our freestanding Dave’s Hot Chicken in Sumter and Dave’s Hot Chicken in Florence. The sequence below is the one we plan South Carolina jobs around.

Who reviews your restaurant

Start with whether the property is inside city limits. If it is, the city’s building and zoning offices review it. If not, the county does. Addresses in South Carolina often carry a city name even when the parcel sits in unincorporated county land, so confirm the jurisdiction from the county’s parcel records before preparing anything.

The technical rules are set statewide. The South Carolina Building Codes Council adopted the 2021 international codes with South Carolina changes, effective January 1, 2023, and every city and county enforces them. The Council has since moved to adopt the 2024 editions, so confirm which edition your office applies on the date you file. What changes from town to town is the paperwork, and much of that belongs to the contractor and the owner.

Zoning is local too. A restaurant moving into an existing shopping center unit is usually a permitted use and a quick check. A new freestanding building, a drive-thru or site changes bring in site plan review, and a driveway on a state highway needs state transportation approval as well. Our drive-thru design guide covers what reviewers look at on the site.

New buildings: the shell comes first

In a new shopping center or outparcel building, the restaurant’s permit depends on the developer’s approvals. On one fit-out in a new outparcel building, the county would not accept the restaurant’s submission until two things happened: the developer’s plat was recorded and the base building permit for the shell was issued.

The new building also had no street address yet, so the county had us identify it by tax parcel, with the address assigned later, and the developer had to sign the application as property owner. If you are leasing in a building that is still going up, ask the landlord where the plat, the shell permit and the address stand before you set an opening date. Our guide to malls and shopping centers covers landlord approvals in more detail.

Step 1: Filing, plan approval and the building permit

These are three different moments, and an opening date should be built on the last one, not the first.

  • Filing. The application is accepted only when it is complete, including the contractor and owner items below.
  • Plan approval. The building department reviews the drawings and approves them, sometimes after comments. In one upstate city, plan review was approved first and the building permit application followed as a separate step.
  • Issuance. The permit is issued once plans are approved, fees are paid and the office’s other conditions are met. Work starts after issuance.

Plan on a South Carolina licensed architect. S.C. Code Section 40-3-290 exempts some buildings under three stories and 5,000 square feet, but not assembly occupancies; determine the restaurant’s classification under the applicable code rather than from the presence of seats alone. Its alteration exemption applies to a building already outside the chapter’s coverage, within the stated area, capacity and structural-safety limits (S.C. Code 40-3-290(C)(4)). Our guide on whether you need an architect explains how to confirm an exemption before relying on it. Some departments also want the mechanical, electrical and plumbing drawings sealed by the engineers who designed them. That was the case in the same upstate city, so ask early and make sure the engineers hold South Carolina licenses.

Departments also ask for the estimated cost of construction, because it sets the permit fee. In Sumter, the permit office required the total estimated cost of construction to calculate the final building permit fee.

Step 2: The contractor’s paperwork

This is the part of a South Carolina filing the architect cannot supply. On our Sumter store, the permit submission needed:

  • The general contractor’s state license and its local business license
  • A fully signed contract between the property owner and the general contractor
  • A full list of subcontractors, with the value of each one’s work
  • The plumbing subcontractor’s information and license class

None of that comes from the architect, and most of it does not exist until the owner has hired a contractor. Choose the contractor early, and have the contract signed by the time the drawings are sealed. We put these items on the kickoff list so the owner sees them on day one.

Step 3: Decide what to keep, one item at a time

Reusing existing equipment can save money, but it does not automatically remove it from design, review or inspection. Each item needs its own answer. Is the rooftop unit sized for the new kitchen and seating? Do the hood and make-up air (fresh air replacing what the hood pulls out) match the new cooking line? Can the existing fire alarm devices stay where the new layout puts walls? Whether reused equipment needs its own trade permit is a question for the local building office, so we ask it in writing before the drawings are final. Our guide to second-generation restaurant space explains what a former restaurant can and cannot save you.

Step 4: Grease and the sewer provider

The state’s own planning guide says the sewer provider decides whether a grease trap or interceptor is required. In a former restaurant, the provider may also want the existing unit checked. In one upstate city, the sewer department required the installed grease control device to be serviced and cleaned before opening and permitting. Ask the sewer provider early whether the existing unit is acceptable at all. Our guide to kitchen hoods, grease and gas covers sizing and placement.

Step 5: The state food permit

Restaurants in South Carolina get their food permit from the state, not a county health department. When the former health and environmental agency was split in July 2024, retail food safety moved to the South Carolina Department of Agriculture, which takes applications online under Regulation 61-25.

The department’s planning guide separates the steps:

  • Application. Submit at least 30 calendar days before the planned opening. For a new restaurant, it includes the menu, the proposed layout, finish schedules and the equipment list with makes, models and locations.
  • Pre-operational inspection. Request it at least 14 calendar days ahead. The state inspects the finished kitchen against the regulation.
  • Permit. Issued only when the restaurant passes that inspection.

The application needs operator information that only the owner has, so we put it on the kickoff list with the contractor paperwork. The guide also points hood questions to the local fire, mechanical and building officials. Our health department plan review guide covers what a food application usually asks for.

Inspections, the CO and the business license

Each trade permit is inspected as the work goes in. In one upstate city, the building office told us no separate occupancy inspection was needed because every inspection would be done through the fit-out permits, though the occupancy permit still had its own application.

The order at the end matters. There, the city issued the certificate of occupancy once the fit-out was complete and then told its business license office the business could be licensed. Plan your opening date around that sequence, not around the last day of construction. Our certificate of occupancy guide covers the closing steps.

A checklist before you sign the lease

  • Confirm from parcel records whether the site is in the city or the county.
  • In a new building, ask where the plat, the shell permit and the street address stand.
  • Hire the contractor early and get the owner and contractor agreement signed.
  • Make sure your engineers are licensed in South Carolina and can seal their own sheets.
  • Ask the sewer provider what it needs from an existing grease interceptor.
  • Apply for the state food permit at least 30 days before opening.
  • Plan for the business license to follow the certificate of occupancy.

Questions

Common Questions

Who issues the food permit for a restaurant in South Carolina?

The South Carolina Department of Agriculture. Its Retail Food Safety program took over restaurant permitting from the former health and environmental agency in July 2024. It asks for the application at least 30 calendar days before the planned opening and issues the permit after a pre-operational inspection.

Who issues a restaurant building permit in South Carolina?

The city's building department if the property is inside city limits, and the county's building department if it is not. Both enforce the same statewide building codes, but each sets its own application requirements.

Do I need a contractor before filing for a restaurant permit in South Carolina?

In practice, yes. On our South Carolina jobs, building applications have asked for the general contractor's state license and local business license, a signed owner and contractor agreement, and a list of subcontractors with the value of each one's work.

When can I get my business license for a restaurant in South Carolina?

Often not until the end. In one upstate city, the business license could not be completed until the certificate of occupancy was issued, and the certificate came only after construction was finished.

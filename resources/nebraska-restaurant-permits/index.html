Opening a Restaurant in Nebraska: Permits and Approvals

Zelta Design · 5 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

In Nebraska, a restaurant's building permit comes from the city or county that covers the property, fire code plan review goes to the State Fire Marshal unless the city holds delegated authority, and food establishments are permitted by the Nebraska Department of Agriculture. State law requires food plans to be approved before construction, conversion or remodeling, so even a remodel should start by asking the food regulator what it needs.

In Nebraska, a restaurant answers to three kinds of office. The city or county building department reviews the drawings and issues the building permit. Fire code plan review goes to the State Fire Marshal, or to the city’s own fire prevention office in the cities where the State Fire Marshal has handed that authority down. Food establishments are permitted by the Nebraska Department of Agriculture. Zoning, signs and site work are local, and additional approvals depend on the scope.

A new restaurant goes through all three. A remodel of an existing one may go through fewer, depending on what changes, and the guide below separates the two. We are licensed in Nebraska and have taken restaurant remodels through city review there in 2026, and we describe what we saw in specific places rather than as a statewide rule.

Who reviews what in Nebraska

Each of these offices has its own filing system. Identify all of them for your address before the drawings are final.

  • Building: the city, village or county. Nebraska’s state building code is based on the 2018 International Building Code. State law lets a county, city or village enforce a local code if it adopts the state code or one that conforms generally with it, so the edition and procedures vary.
  • Fire: the State Fire Marshal or a delegated city. The State Fire Marshal’s plans division FAQ says all projects involving commercial construction must be submitted for plan review, with exemptions that include projects inside delegated authorities. Its delegated authority list names the cities that review in its place, including Omaha, Lincoln, Bellevue, Grand Island and Norfolk. The same page lists the cities and counties that do their own accessibility review.
  • Food: the Nebraska Department of Agriculture. The department’s food safety program inspects and licenses food establishments, with contracted local health departments in Douglas, Lancaster and Hall counties doing inspections there.

For most restaurant work, the drawings must be sealed by an architect or engineer licensed in Nebraska. The state’s licensing law has some exemptions for small buildings; our guide on whether you need an architect explains how seal rules work.

New restaurant or remodel

What triggers each review depends on the work, so define the scope before you call anyone.

  • A new restaurant in a new building or a converted space needs zoning approval, a building permit, fire code plan review and food plan review before construction, then a food establishment permit before opening.
  • A remodel of an operating restaurant still needs a building permit for most construction. Whether it needs fire or food review depends on what changes: kitchen equipment, hood and suppression, walls, exits or seating.
  • Exterior changes such as new signs, a reworked drive-thru or parking changes usually bring in zoning and a separate sign permit.

Our guide to restaurant remodels explains which changes tend to pull in more review.

Step 1: Filing the building permit

Filing rules vary city by city, and getting them right the first time saves a round trip. What we saw in Bellevue in 2026 shows the kind of detail to ask about.

  • Paper sets. In January 2026, Bellevue’s building department asked for paper copies of the complete set, printed large enough to read easily.
  • The suite number. In April 2026 the same office asked that the application include the bay or suite number and be attached with the plans. In a shopping center, the suite number is how the city ties the permit to your space.
  • Owner information. Applications ask for the construction value and the contractor. We fill in what the drawings can answer and send the owner a list of the rest at the start.

Ask each city these questions before printing anything.

Step 2: Review now, release later

Some Nebraska cities will review drawings before the contractor is chosen but hold the permit until they know who is building it. Plan for that, and the review and bidding can overlap.

In January 2026, Bellevue’s building department confirmed that the application could list the contractor and subcontractors as to be determined at submission. It told us it would fully review the submission in the meantime but would not release the permit until it had the contractor’s information and the job value. File the drawings as soon as they are ready and choose the contractor in parallel. Our permit timeline guide explains how owner decisions like this drive the calendar.

Ask at permit issuance whether the city also requires the contractor to register or hold a local license before inspections can be scheduled.

Step 3: Fire code review

Fire code review is a separate step from the building permit in much of Nebraska. Check the State Fire Marshal’s delegated authority list for your city first.

Outside a delegated city, commercial plans go to the State Fire Marshal’s plans division through its online application. Its FAQ also says shop drawings for fire alarms, sprinklers and range hood suppression are submitted separately by the contractors who install them. Inside a delegated city, the city’s fire prevention office reviews instead. Our guide to restaurant fire protection covers what those reviews look at.

Step 4: Food establishment review

Food plan review applies to more than new buildings. Nebraska Revised Statute 81-2,267 requires plans for the construction, conversion or remodeling of a food establishment to be submitted to the regulatory authority and approved before the work.

City building departments do not route the food review for you, so the owner’s team has to raise it. On a remodel, we raise it at the start of design. For a remodel where the kitchen is not obviously changing, describe the scope to the Department of Agriculture in writing and ask whether it needs plans. Keep whatever answer comes back with the permit file, so the question does not surface for the first time at the opening inspection. Our health department plan review guide covers what a full submission contains when one is needed.

Inspections and opening

The city that issued the permit inspects the work and signs off at the end. The fire reviewer may inspect separately, and the food regulator inspects before a new establishment opens. For a remodel of a store that stays open or reopens quickly, confirm early which inspections each office needs before customers come back.

A checklist before you start

  • Confirm which city or county building department covers the address and how it wants plans delivered.
  • Check the State Fire Marshal’s delegated authority list for your city.
  • Put the bay or suite number on every application and drawing.
  • Choose the contractor in parallel with design, because the permit may wait for the contractor’s information and the job value.
  • Ask the Department of Agriculture in writing whether your scope needs food plan review.
  • Check whether new signs need their own permit.

Questions

Common Questions

Who issues a restaurant building permit in Nebraska?

The city, village or county building department that covers the property. Nebraska law lets local governments adopt the state building code or a code that conforms generally with it, so the edition and the filing rules depend on the address.

Does the State Fire Marshal review restaurant plans?

For commercial construction, yes, unless the project is in one of the cities where the State Fire Marshal has delegated authority, such as Omaha, Lincoln or Bellevue. There, plans go to the city's own fire prevention office instead.

Does a restaurant remodel in Nebraska need food plan review?

Nebraska law requires plans for construction, conversion or remodeling of a food establishment to be approved before the work. Whether a limited refresh counts as remodeling is the regulator's call, so describe the scope to the Department of Agriculture in writing and keep its answer.

Can a Nebraska city review my plans before I have a contractor?

Some will. In January 2026, Bellevue's building department told us it would fully review a submission with the contractor listed as to be determined, but would not release the permit until it had the contractor's information and the job value.

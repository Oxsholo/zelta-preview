Permits by State guide

Opening a Restaurant in West Virginia: Permits and Approvals

Zelta Design · 6 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

In West Virginia, a restaurant usually deals with three separate reviewers: the county or town that enforces the State Building Code, the State Fire Marshal's plan review, and the local health department. Where a county or town has zoning, it often has to clear before the building permit issues. Which offices apply depends on the parcel and the scope, so confirm them first.

Restaurant approvals in West Virginia run on up to three separate tracks, and which offices apply depends on the parcel. Under state law, the State Building Code takes effect only in the counties and towns that adopt it, and enforcing it is the job of that local government. So the building permit comes from the county or town building office. The State Fire Marshal’s Plans Review division runs its own review of the plans and of kitchen hood suppression systems. The local health department permits and inspects food service establishments after reviewing the kitchen.

Zoning sits in front of the building permit where the county or town has it. Additional approvals depend on scope: a new driveway on a state road, a sign, or a well or septic system can each add a step. We are licensed in West Virginia and our work there has been in the Eastern Panhandle, so the local examples below come from Jefferson County.

The tracks and how they depend on each other

There is one gate and then three parallel reviews. Zoning comes first where it applies. Once it clears, the building permit, the State Fire Marshal review and the health review can all be in review at once.

  1. Zoning (county or town). Confirms the use and the site. In Jefferson County it is a prerequisite to the building permit.
  2. Building permit (county or town building office). Reviews the full construction set.
  3. State Fire Marshal plan review (state). A separate online filing.
  4. Food service plan review and permit (local health department). Reviews the kitchen and permits the restaurant to operate.

The reviews are separate applications, not independent ones. The building office will not issue a permit until zoning has cleared, and on our Jefferson County project the building reviewer asked us to show where the fire and food reviews stood. No office files another office’s application for you, so each filing needs a named owner from the first week.

Step 1: Find the right jurisdiction

Start with the parcel, not the mailing address. Building code enforcement is local wherever a county or town has adopted the code, and the boundaries matter.

Jefferson County is a good example. Its commercial permit instructions say the county’s building permit requirements apply only to the unincorporated county, not to the five incorporated towns: Charles Town, Ranson, Harpers Ferry, Bolivar and Shepherdstown. A mailing address can mislead. One of our Jefferson County projects carried a town’s name in its mailing address but sat in the unincorporated county, so the county reviewed it. We confirm which office covers the exact parcel before anything is filed.

Step 2: Zoning and the site plan

In Jefferson County, zoning is the gate. On a recent shopping center fit-out there, the zoning certificate went in first because the county treats it as a prerequisite to the building permit.

The county’s commercial permit instructions also ask for a site plan showing building setbacks and distances to property lines, and say the permit issues only after the site plan, where applicable, is approved by the Planning Commission. That is a Jefferson County requirement. Other counties and towns set their own, so ask the local office what it wants, even for interior work. If the landlord has a recent site plan for the center, ask for it early.

Step 3: The building permit

The county or town building office reviews the whole construction set and issues the permit. Jefferson County’s instructions ask for drawings with a West Virginia architect’s or engineer’s seal “as applicable.” For most restaurant work that means a sealed set, but seal rules have exemptions; our guide on whether you need an architect explains them.

Reviewers read the whole set, not just the floor plan. Before filing, make sure the owner, landlord and contractor can answer these, because the drawings depend on them:

  • Fuel. Natural gas or propane, and if propane, where the tank will sit.
  • Electrical service. Whether the existing panel and service can carry the new kitchen. The engineer shows this by calculation.
  • Water. The pressure available at the meter, which the plumbing design has to work with.
  • Roof. Whether new rooftop equipment needs structural support. When the framing is hidden until demolition, the check may close in the field; our rooftop units guide covers a West Virginia example.
  • Fire and food. Confirmation that the State Fire Marshal and health filings are in.
  • Contractor paperwork. On our Jefferson County job, the county needed the general contractor’s West Virginia license and insurance certificate before it would issue the permit.

When comments come back, they are answered with a response letter and a revised sealed set. Our guide to answering plan review comments explains how.

Step 4: The State Fire Marshal

The State Fire Marshal reviews restaurant plans as its own filing. Its Plans Review division reviews architectural, mechanical, electrical and plumbing plans and specialized systems such as fire alarms, sprinklers and hood suppression. Submissions go through its online Plan Review Request Portal, and it no longer accepts paper.

On our West Virginia restaurant projects, this review was separate from the county building permit and ran at the same time as the county and health reviews. Decide at the start who files it, whether owner, contractor or architect, and keep the filing confirmation with the project record. The kitchen hood’s suppression system is usually drawn by the hood or suppression vendor, not the architect. Our guide to sprinklers, fire alarms and the fire marshal explains how these pieces fit together.

Step 5: The health department

Food service in West Virginia is permitted and inspected by the local health department, under the state’s food establishment rule. The state’s form list includes a plan review report for new or remodeled food service establishments, and the local department reviews the kitchen before it permits the restaurant to open.

In Jefferson County, the health department invoiced its fee to the operator after the packet went in, so the operator should pay it as soon as it arrives. The packet needs things only the operator has, such as the menu and food handling details, so we list them at the start of the job. Our health department plan review guide covers what goes in it.

Grease interceptors

Grease handling shapes the plumbing sheets and sometimes the site. A grease interceptor is a tank that catches kitchen grease. Whether it is a compact unit inside or a large tank buried outside depends on the plumbing code and on the local sewer utility, and an outside tank can mean digging up part of the parking lot. We confirm the requirement with the utility before the plumbing drawings are final. Our guide to kitchen hoods, grease and gas has more.

Inspections and opening

Each office inspects its own work. The building office inspects construction and signs off at the end. The fire side and the health department do their own inspections, and the health permit has to be in place before the doors open. Track all of them to the end, not just the building permit. Our certificate of occupancy guide covers closeout.

A short West Virginia checklist

  1. Confirm whether the parcel is in a town or in the unincorporated county.
  2. Ask the zoning office what it needs and whether zoning must clear before the building permit.
  3. Get a site plan, or budget for one, if the local office requires it.
  4. Settle the fuel, electrical service and water pressure before the drawings are final.
  5. Name who files the State Fire Marshal review and the health packet, and file both alongside the building permit.
  6. Have the operator pay the health fee as soon as it is invoiced.
  7. Have the contractor’s West Virginia license and insurance certificate ready before issuance.
  8. Confirm the grease interceptor requirement with the sewer utility.

Questions

Common Questions

Does the State Fire Marshal review restaurant plans in West Virginia?

The State Fire Marshal's Plans Review division reviews architectural, mechanical, electrical and plumbing plans and hood suppression systems, filed through its online portal. On our West Virginia restaurant projects it has been its own filing, separate from the county building permit, and it ran at the same time as the other reviews.

Who issues a restaurant building permit in West Virginia?

The county or town that has adopted the State Building Code and enforces it locally. In Jefferson County, for example, the county permits office covers only the unincorporated county, and the five incorporated towns run their own permitting. Confirm the jurisdiction from the parcel before you file.

Can the West Virginia reviews run at the same time?

Mostly. In Jefferson County the zoning certificate had to clear before the building permit, so it went in first. After that, the building permit, the State Fire Marshal review and the health review can run in parallel, because each office reviews only its own filing.

Do I need a site plan for an interior restaurant fit-out?

Ask the local office. Jefferson County's commercial permit instructions call for a site plan showing setbacks and distances to property lines. Other counties and towns set their own rules.

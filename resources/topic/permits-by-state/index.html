Who reviews what, side by side

PlaceBuilding permitKitchen plan reviewFire reviewWorth knowing
ConnecticutThe town or city building official, under the statewide building codeThe town health department or a regional health districtThe local fire marshal reviews plans before the building permit is issuedThere is no county layer, and the health district may be based in another town
FloridaThe city building department, or the county on unincorporated landThe state: business regulation for most restaurants, agriculture for bakeries and marketsThe local fire official of the county, city or special fire districtExterior doors, windows and storefronts need Florida product approval; late swaps can restart review
MarylandUsually the county, or the city inside some incorporated citiesThe local health department, before construction startsThe State Fire Marshal or a county fire office, depending on the countyIn Prince George's, an older nonconforming restaurant must prove its use never stopped over 180 straight days
New JerseyThe town's construction official, under the statewide Uniform Construction CodeThe local health authority: the town, the county or a regional health commissionThe fire subcode official in the town's construction officeZoning and board approvals come first; the town holds the construction permit until they are met
New York (outside NYC)The city, town or village; the county or state if it opted outA county health department in many counties, a state district office elsewhereOften a town or county fire marshal, especially on Long IslandSmall exterior changes, like a new menu board or restriping, can need planning board approval
New York CityThe Department of Buildings, filed online by a New York licensed architect or engineerThe Health Department, which issues the food service establishment permitThe Fire Department, for hood suppression, alarms, sprinklers and rooftop equipmentThe landlord must confirm the filing online; a tenant cannot supply it
North CarolinaThe city or county inspections departmentThe state Plan Review Unit for chains of ten or more; otherwise the countyReviewed locally, often by the fire marshalWhether the state or the county reviews the kitchen depends on chain size, not location
OhioA state-certified city, county or township department; otherwise the stateThe local health district: a city health department or county board of healthAsk the building departmentA small village may not review commercial work, so the permit can come from the state
PennsylvaniaThe municipality's code official, a third-party agency, a regional office or the stateThe state Department of Agriculture, or a county or local health departmentAsk the building departmentThe mailing address often names a borough when the parcel sits in a township
South CarolinaThe city inside city limits, the county outside themThe South Carolina Department of Agriculture, which took over restaurant permits in 2024Reviewed locally; ask the building department who reviews fire plansThe business license often cannot be finished until the certificate of occupancy is issued
TennesseeThe local code office in exempt jurisdictions; elsewhere the state, for buildings meeting its triggersThe state Department of Health, or the county in Davidson, Hamilton, Knox, Madison and ShelbyThe same route as building plans: the local code office or the State Fire Marshal's OfficeThe general contractor may have to pull the permit or supply documents during review
VirginiaThe building department of the county, independent city or townThe local health department, before construction startsA local fire marshal's office usually reviews sprinkler and hood suppression plansA building permit alone does not open the restaurant; fire, health and occupancy close separately
West VirginiaThe county or town building office that has adopted the State Building CodeThe local health departmentThe State Fire Marshal's Plans Review division, as a separate online filingWhere the county or town has zoning, it often must clear before the building permit

Each row summarizes its guide. Rules change; confirm with the office that covers your address.

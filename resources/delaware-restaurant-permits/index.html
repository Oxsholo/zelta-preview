Opening a Restaurant in Delaware: Permits and Approvals

Zelta Design · 4 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

In Delaware, the county or town reviews the site plan and issues the building permit, the Office of the State Fire Marshal reviews fire protection on its own track, and the Division of Public Health's Office of Food Protection reviews the kitchen. County amendments to the building code matter: on a new restaurant building in New Castle County, the reviewer applied the county's automatic door and snow load amendments.

In Delaware, a restaurant goes through three separate reviewers. The county, or the town if the site is inside an incorporated town with its own building department, reviews the site plan and issues the building permit. The Office of the State Fire Marshal reviews fire protection on its own track. The Division of Public Health’s Office of Food Protection reviews the kitchen. None of them waits for the others, so all three should start together.

We are licensed in Delaware and have designed new drive-thru restaurant buildings and fit-outs in New Castle and Kent counties. This guide covers the statewide offices and what New Castle County asked for on the projects we took through review there.

Who reviews what in Delaware

Three offices touch nearly every restaurant. Confirm at the start whether your site is in a town or in the unincorporated county.

  • Site and building: the county or town. In New Castle County, building permits are filed through the county’s ePlans system, and the Department of Land Use handles both the land development plans and the building permit.
  • Fire protection: the Office of the State Fire Marshal. It has separate building plan applications for new structures and for tenant fit-outs, renovations and changes of use, plus its own site plan review.
  • Food service: the Office of Food Protection. The state’s business guide tells new food businesses to contact the Food Protection Office before construction to follow its plan review procedure.

Step 1: The site plan

A new restaurant building starts with a site plan, and in Delaware two agencies review it.

For a drive-thru restaurant we designed near Wilmington in 2023, the civil engineer’s plan went to New Castle County as a minor redevelopment plan: combining three tax parcels, demolishing the old buildings and building the restaurant. The record plan’s notes show what the county attaches to an approval:

  • the landscape plan becomes part of the recorded plan;
  • site improvements are tied to a recorded land development improvement agreement backed by a performance guarantee, a bond or similar security;
  • sidewalks shown on the plan must be built before the certificate of occupancy;
  • drainage and stormwater follow the county drainage code, and fire lanes and hydrants follow the state fire protection regulations.

The State Fire Marshal reviews the same site for fire access and water supply, and a site plan must be approved before a new building can be. Our pad site guide covers the civil side.

Step 2: The fire flow test

If a public water utility serves the site, the State Fire Marshal wants a water flow test from the past twelve months with the site plan. The sprinkler engineer also needs it to size the system.

On a New Castle County restaurant in 2021, the water utility ran the test about six weeks after it was requested, using two hydrants to measure the static pressure, the flow and the residual pressure. Request the test as soon as the site is under contract, because the sprinkler design waits for it. Our fire protection guide explains what the numbers feed.

Step 3: The building permit and county amendments

The building code is the same national model code most states use, but New Castle County amends it, and its reviewers apply those amendments. The county’s January 2022 comments on a new drive-thru restaurant building we designed included:

  • An automatic door. The reviewer cited the county’s amendment requiring newly built places of public accommodation, a term taken from Delaware’s equal accommodations law, to have an automatic or power-assisted door at each main public entrance.
  • A higher snow load. The county’s amendment set the ground snow load at 25 pounds per square foot. That value drives the roof’s drift loads, so the structural engineer should design to it from the start.
  • Fire-rated assemblies. The construction type on the code sheet required one-hour rated roof and bearing wall assemblies, and the reviewer wanted the tested assembly listings named on the drawings.
  • Slab edge insulation. Under the 2018 energy code, insulation at the slab edge has to extend down from the top of the slab, or its value comes out of the energy compliance calculation.

Check the current county code for the amendments that apply on your filing date. Our accessibility guide covers entrances and accessible routes in more depth.

Step 4: Food protection and opening

The Office of Food Protection reviews the kitchen before construction. Expect to provide the menu, the equipment schedule and scaled floor plans, and a pre-opening inspection before the food permit is issued. Our health department plan review guide covers the packet.

At the end, each office closes its own approval: county or town inspections and the certificate of occupancy, the State Fire Marshal’s inspection of the fire protection systems, and the food permit. See the certificate of occupancy.

A checklist before you sign the lease

  • Confirm whether the site is in a town or the unincorporated county.
  • Ask whether a land development or redevelopment plan is needed, and who prepares it.
  • Request the fire flow test from the water utility early.
  • Start the State Fire Marshal and food protection reviews alongside the building permit.
  • Ask the building office which county amendments apply, especially entrances and snow load.

Questions

Common Questions

Does the State Fire Marshal review restaurant plans in Delaware?

Yes. The Office of the State Fire Marshal has its own applications for new buildings and for tenant fit-outs, renovations and changes of use, and it must approve the site plan before it approves a new building.

Do I need a fire flow test for a Delaware restaurant?

Usually, if there is a public water utility. The State Fire Marshal asks for a water flow test from the past twelve months with the site plan, and the sprinkler design depends on it.

Are there county building code amendments in Delaware?

Yes. In 2022, New Castle County's reviewer required an automatic door at the main entrance of a new restaurant and a ground snow load of 25 pounds per square foot, both under the county's amendments.

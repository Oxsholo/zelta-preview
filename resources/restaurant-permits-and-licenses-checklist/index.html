Restaurant Permits and Licenses Checklist: Every Approval

Zelta Design · 5 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

Opening a restaurant takes two sets of approvals. The building side covers zoning, any board approval, the building permit, health plan review, fire review, signs and the certificate of occupancy; the business side covers your tax registrations, business license, food manager certificate and any liquor license. Which office issues each one varies by address, so build the list for your site before you sign the lease.

To open a restaurant you need two sets of approvals. The first is about the building: zoning, any planning or zoning board approval, the building permit and its trade permits, health department plan review, fire review, sign permits and, at the end, a certificate of occupancy. The second is about the business: tax registrations, a business license where your city requires one, a certified food protection manager and, if you serve alcohol, a liquor license.

The names and the offices change with the address. Across our own projects, one Pennsylvania store needed a township land use permit, building review by a private code agency the township uses, and a food license from the state agriculture department: three offices for one small restaurant. A Pittsburgh store needed only building and health permits, with no zoning step, which we confirmed with the zoning official. Build your list for your site, in this order.

Stage 1: Before you sign the lease or buy

Confirm the restaurant use is allowed and find out which approvals the site will need. This is the cheapest point to find a problem.

  • Zoning check. Is a restaurant, a fast food restaurant or a drive-thru allowed here, and does it need a hearing? See will this site work.
  • Existing use. For a former restaurant, has the use stayed active? See taking over a former restaurant.
  • Landlord approval. Most leases require the landlord to approve your drawings, signs and rooftop work. See the lease checklist.
  • Brand site approval. Franchise brands approve the site and the test fit before design goes further. See brand design approvals.

Stage 2: Land use approvals

If the site, the use, the signs or the building need relief from the zoning rules, a board decides before the building permit. Plan for these first because they set the schedule.

  • Zoning permit or certificate. On a West Virginia store, the zoning certificate had to go in first because it was a prerequisite to the building permit, and the application needed only the property owner’s signature.
  • Site plan review for new buildings, drive-thrus, parking changes and additions. See building on a pad site.
  • Special permits and variances when the use, a drive-thru or the signs do not meet the code.
  • Historic district review where it applies. See restaurants in historic buildings.
  • Road agencies. On a New Jersey Taco Bell, the township told us the site sat on a county road, so an application to the county was needed as well.

Stage 3: Design approvals and the building permit

The building permit is issued against sealed drawings, and several other reviews run alongside it. Some must clear before the building permit is released.

  • Building permit, with electrical, plumbing, mechanical and sometimes gas permits. The drawings must be sealed by an architect or engineer licensed in your state. See what’s in the permit set and why licensing matters.
  • Health department plan review of the kitchen, equipment and finishes. See health department plan review.
  • Fire review of sprinklers, the fire alarm and the hood’s fire suppression system, often by a separate office. See fire protection.
  • Grease and sewer approvals. Some cities run their own grease programs with their own applications. See kitchen engineering.
  • Sign permits, separate from the building permit in most towns. See signage permits.
  • Accessibility is checked inside the building review. See ADA requirements for restaurants.

The order between these varies. On a co-branded renovation in Hagerstown, Maryland, health and fire approval were needed before full building approval could be granted. On a West Virginia job, the city needed a site plan with every submission: zoning, building, utility and fire. Ask the building department at the start which approvals gate the permit.

Stage 4: During construction

Once the permit is issued, inspections run each trade, and the contractors carry paperwork of their own.

  • Contractor registration and licensing. Some cities require the contractor to register or hold a local license before inspections can be scheduled. In North Little Rock, Arkansas, the general contractor pulls the permit, and many cities add their own business license or bond requirements.
  • Trade permits. Some offices issue one permit and then expect each trade contractor to file its own. On a restaurant on tribal land in Connecticut, the land use approval came with a trade permit template that each contractor submits for itself.
  • Inspections at each stage, and revisions for any change made in the field. See what your architect does during construction.

Stage 5: Before you open

The last approvals come from the building, fire and health offices, and each runs on its own schedule.

  • Final inspections for every trade, plus fire acceptance tests.
  • Health inspection and permit to operate. On a Jimmy John’s in western Maryland, the health office’s opening inspection list included self-closing restroom doors and a copy of the fire marshal’s approval.
  • Certificate of occupancy, or your town’s equivalent. See how to get a certificate of occupancy.

The business licenses

These belong to the owner, not the design team, but several depend on the building approvals above.

  • Federal tax ID. An employer identification number from the IRS, which the IRS issues free online.
  • State registrations for sales tax and payroll, through your state’s agencies.
  • Local business license. Many cities require one. In Anderson, South Carolina, the city requires a license to operate a business, and in some places the business license waits for the certificate of occupancy.
  • Certified food protection manager. The FDA’s model Food Code, which states adapt into their own rules, calls for at least one manager to hold a food protection manager certification, and health departments ask for the certificates during plan review.
  • Liquor license if you serve alcohol. The SBA’s licenses and permits guide lists alcohol among federally regulated activities, with local alcohol beverage control boards issuing retail licenses. Check your state’s rules early, before the opening date is set.

The checklist

  1. Zoning confirmation for a restaurant (and drive-thru) at this address
  2. Restaurant use still active, for a former restaurant
  3. Landlord approval of drawings, signs and roof work
  4. Brand approval of the site, test fit and design
  5. Board approvals: site plan, special permit, variance, historic review
  6. County or state road approvals, if the site fronts one
  7. Building permit and trade permits
  8. Health department plan approval
  9. Fire approvals: sprinklers, alarm, hood suppression
  10. Grease or sewer program approval, where one exists
  11. Sign permits
  12. Contractor registrations and licenses
  13. Final inspections and fire acceptance tests
  14. Health inspection and permit to operate
  15. Certificate of occupancy or equivalent
  16. Tax ID, state registrations and local business license
  17. Certified food protection manager
  18. Liquor license, if serving alcohol

For how long each stage takes, see how long a restaurant build-out takes.

Questions

Common Questions

What permits do I need to open a restaurant?
Usually a zoning approval, a building permit with its trade permits, health department plan approval and a permit to operate, fire approvals for sprinklers, alarms and the hood system, a sign permit and a certificate of occupancy. On the business side you need tax registrations, a business license where your city requires one, a certified food manager and a liquor license if you serve alcohol.
Which restaurant permit should I apply for first?
Zoning, because it decides whether a restaurant can operate at the address at all. Some towns will not accept a building permit application until the zoning certificate is issued, and a board hearing, if one is needed, usually sets the schedule for everything after it.
Does the architect get the restaurant permits?
The architect prepares and seals the drawings and usually files or supports the building permit and plan review submissions. Business licenses, tax registrations, the food manager certificate and liquor licensing belong to the owner, and contractors usually pull their own trade permits.

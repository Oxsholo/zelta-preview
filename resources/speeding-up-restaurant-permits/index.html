Can You Speed Up a Restaurant Permit? What Actually Works

Zelta Design · 5 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

Sometimes, but only one review at a time. Some cities sell an expedited building plan review, New York City lets the architect professionally certify qualifying filings, and Florida lets an owner hire a private provider for plan review and inspections. Each can move its own review sooner, while every other required approval still controls when the permit issues. Filing a complete package once, in the right order, is the habit that helps every review.

You can sometimes speed up a restaurant permit, but usually one review at a time. Some building departments sell a faster plan review for a fee. New York City lets a licensed architect professionally certify qualifying filings. Florida lets an owner hire a private provider for plan review and inspections. Each can move its own review sooner. None of them shortens the other reviews your site needs, and the permit still issues only when every required approval is in.

Most restaurants need several approvals, each with its own reviewer (see the restaurant build-out timeline). So before paying for any program, find out which review is actually the slow one. This guide covers the programs, what each one changes, and the filing habits that help every review.

Paid expedited and after-hours review

Some cities offer a faster building plan review for an extra fee. It helps when the building review is the slow part, and only then.

Columbus, Ohio is a clear example. Its expedited after-hours building plan review form offers review of commercial plans outside business hours for a fee on top of the normal permit fee. The form sets the limits plainly:

  • A plans examiner decides whether the project qualifies. If the scope or examiner availability does not allow it, the application goes back to regular review.
  • It covers building plan review only. The permit cannot be issued until other applicable reviews, such as zoning and site compliance, are approved.
  • Comment responses must stay on the same path. Responses and revisions are also reviewed after hours, at an added fee, to keep the separate track.

On a Columbus project, the signed request form was attached to the portal submission alongside the construction documents, rather than sent separately.

What it moves: the building plan review. What still controls issuance: zoning, site, health, fire, historic or any other review your site needs. If one of those is the gate, a faster building review does not move the opening date.

Professional certification in New York City

In New York City, the applicant of record may, based on the owner’s consent, professionally certify that a filing complies instead of requesting a Department of Buildings plan review. The Department says a professionally certified application may be audited, and a failed audit sends a notice of objections to the applicant and the owner, the same as a failed plan review.

So the choice is a trade. Certification can reach approval sooner, but the risk of objections moves to after approval, sometimes after work has started. It suits straightforward fit-outs with a settled design. A job with a change of use, a landmark, a complex exit layout or a design still moving is often better served by a full plan exam. We make that call job by job. See opening a restaurant in New York City for the rest of the city’s process.

What it moves: the building department’s plan approval. What still controls issuance: other city agencies, landmarks where they apply, and the audit risk afterward.

Private providers in Florida

Under section 553.791 of the Florida Statutes (2026), the owner, or the owner’s contractor with the owner’s written authorization, may use a private provider for building code plan review, inspections or both. The local building official must be notified in writing on the required form. The building official still issues the permit and the certificate of occupancy, and the statute sets deadlines for the official to act. The local jurisdiction must reduce its permit fee by the cost savings it realizes, with minimum reductions set in the statute.

It works best when decided before filing, so the drawings, application and provider paperwork go in together. On one Orlando area store, the private provider letters were owner-side paperwork the owner had to sign, which is worth putting on the schedule early. It covers the building code only. Zoning, utilities and the state food license follow their own processes (see opening a restaurant in Florida).

What it moves: building code plan review and inspections. What still controls issuance: zoning, utility and other non-building reviews, and the building official’s own completeness check.

Third-party code agencies in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, a municipality that enforces the state construction code may do so through its own staff or by retaining one or more third-party agencies. That choice belongs to the municipality. Where it has retained only one agency, you cannot shop for a faster one. What you can do is confirm early which office reviews your address, and whether the municipality offers more than one agency. See opening a restaurant in Pennsylvania.

Permit expediters

An expediter files applications, tracks them and works the counter on your behalf. That can save the owner real time where portals are heavy or counters are busy. It does not change how fast the reviewer reads, and an expediter is only as fast as the package they receive.

On an Orlando area store filed by an expediter, the county portal tracked each sheet by its number, so when a revised set renumbered sheets, every file had to be matched to the right slot by its content before upload. We send expediters a sheet list and revision notes with the drawings so they are never guessing. See who does what on a restaurant project.

Early start permits

Some cities allow limited work, such as interior demolition, before the full permit. Columbus lists an advance construction start in its 2026 development fee schedule, and on a Columbus project we included an advance start request in the permit package to begin demolition before full approval. These routes are narrow: the building official decides what work qualifies, and structural or fire-rated work is generally excluded. Ask your city what its version covers before planning around it.

Habits that help every review

These are the practices we use on every filing.

  • File complete the first time. A set cannot go in while signatures, the contractor, the construction value or the menu are missing. The owner checklist lists each item and when it is needed.
  • Copy the scope exactly. We copy the scope of work from the drawing title sheet word for word into every application, so the form and the drawings never disagree.
  • File in parallel where the order allows. Health and building can often go in together. Where one gates the other, file the gate first.
  • Ask for a written “not required”. When a review may not apply, a short written answer from the agency closes the question for everyone.
  • Answer comments completely. A partial answer costs a full cycle. See how to answer plan review comments.

Questions

Common Questions

Does paying for expedited plan review speed up the whole permit?
Not by itself. Paid programs usually cover the building plan review only. In Columbus, Ohio, for example, the city's form says the permit cannot be issued until other reviews such as zoning and site compliance are approved.
What is professional certification in New York City?
A route where the architect or engineer, with the owner's consent, certifies that the plans comply instead of waiting for a Department of Buildings plan exam. The city says certified applications may be audited, and a failed audit produces objections the drawings must answer.
Is a permit expediter the same as expedited review?
No. An expediter is a consultant who files and tracks applications and works the counter for you. Expedited review is a program run by the building department itself. An expediter saves legwork but cannot make a reviewer read faster.
What is the most useful thing an owner can do to get a permit sooner?
Have the owner items ready before the drawings are sealed: signatures, legal entity, landlord authorization, contractor details and construction value. A finished set cannot be filed while any of them is missing.

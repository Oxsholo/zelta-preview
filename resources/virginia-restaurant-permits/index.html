Opening a Restaurant in Virginia: Permits and Approvals

Zelta Design · 6 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

In Virginia, the building department of the county, city or town that covers the parcel issues the building permit under the statewide Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code. The local health department reviews the kitchen plans under state food rules, and a local fire marshal's office usually reviews sprinkler and hood suppression plans. The code is the same everywhere, but each locality runs its own process, and other approvals depend on the scope of the work.

Opening a restaurant in Virginia runs through the local government that covers your parcel, with three main reviewers. The building department of the county, independent city or town reviews the construction drawings and issues the building permit. The local health department reviews the kitchen. A local fire marshal’s office usually reviews the sprinkler and hood suppression plans. Additional approvals, such as site plan review, signs or a new driveway, depend on the scope of the work.

The building code is uniform. The process is not. Northern Virginia counties run large online systems with their own rules about uploads, applicants and closeout. We are licensed in Virginia and have worked on restaurant fit-outs in Fairfax and Prince William counties. The details below are local examples from those two counties, not a statewide sequence, so confirm each step with the locality that covers your parcel.

How the Virginia system is built

Virginia has one building code for the whole state, and local building departments enforce it. The code is the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, adopted by the state Board of Housing and Community Development and administered by the Department of Housing and Community Development. Under the code’s enforcement rules, the local building department enforces it for construction, and small towns may leave enforcement to their county.

That means the technical rules are predictable from county to county. The reviewers, portals and habits are not. Drawings for most restaurant fit-outs are sealed by an architect or engineer licensed in Virginia, but Code of Virginia § 54.1-402 lists exemptions, such as some interior remodeling that changes neither the occupant load nor the exits. Our guide to whether you need an architect explains how to check your scope.

Step 1: Find the locality

Start with the parcel record, not the mailing address. Virginia’s independent cities are separate from the counties around them, so a parcel is in a county or a city, never both. Towns are different: a town sits inside a county and may handle some approvals itself while the county handles others. Confirm which office handles zoning, building, health and fire for that exact parcel.

Step 2: Zoning

Zoning in Virginia is local, and each county or city decides how it connects to the building permit. Taking over a space that was already a restaurant, or a retail unit in a shopping center zoned for commercial use, is usually the simplest case. A new building, a drive-thru or a change to the site brings in site plan review and sometimes a public hearing.

The two counties we know best handle zoning differently. In Fairfax County, the zoning reviewers are one of the offices that review the commercial alteration permit package, so an interior fit-out does not need a separate zoning approval first. In Prince William County, the county’s page for tenant space alterations says zoning approval is required before you apply for the building permit.

Step 3: The building permit

The building permit comes from the local building department, and each one has its own intake rules. In Fairfax County, a commercial fit-out is filed online in PLUS, the county’s permitting system. One package goes in and is routed to intake, building plan review, site, the fire marshal, wastewater and zoning. Several Fairfax rules shape how we prepare it.

The coversheet is its own file. The county’s building plan coversheet is uploaded separately from the coversheet in the drawing set, as the same county page explains.

The contractor can come later, but not too late. The statewide code’s permit rules, section 108, let the design professional apply, but proof of the contractor’s Virginia license must be furnished before the permit is issued. In Fairfax County we file as the applicant and add the licensed contractor before issuance, and the county links to the state’s contractor license lookup.

Use a real construction cost. Under the county’s fee schedule, the permit fee for commercial alterations, including tenant fit-outs, is a percentage of the estimated cost of construction, and the permittee must provide verifiable detail of that cost. A placeholder number causes trouble later.

Answer every request quickly. An application cannot move while it waits on the owner’s side for landlord details, entity information or the construction cost. We list those items at the start and keep every online record moving until it closes.

In Prince William County, the building permit comes from the Building Development Division of Development Services, and building plans go in through the county’s online portal. The county’s Fire Marshal’s Office runs its own review of fire protection plans, which we cover below.

Step 4: Health

Food service plan review in Virginia goes to the local health department, and it has to finish before construction starts. The state food rules, 12VAC5-421-3600, require plans to be submitted for review and approval before a food establishment is built, converted from another use or, when the department asks for plans, remodeled.

In Fairfax County, the health filing is its own application, not part of the building package. The building package lists the Health Department as a reviewer only when the property is on a well or septic system. The Fairfax County Health Department’s page for new food establishments says to start the building permit application with Land Development Services, then file the food establishment application through the county’s PLUS portal. Construction cannot begin until the Health Department has completed its plan review. Both filings live in the same online system, but they are separate records with separate reviews.

The health application needs the operator’s details, menu and equipment information, which only the operator has. That is why we list what is needed at the start and file the health application as soon as the building application is in. Our health department plan review guide covers the packet.

Step 5: The fire marshal

Fire review in Northern Virginia is a separate track, and it is easy to treat as the contractor’s problem. In Fairfax County, the Office of the Fire Marshal’s Engineering Plans Review Branch reviews sprinkler, fire alarm and range hood plans, and its installation permits go to the licensed contractor who installs the system. In Prince William County, the Fire Marshal’s Office reviews fire protection plans through its ePlans process.

That makes the filings a shared responsibility. The sprinkler and hood suppression subcontractors prepare and file their own drawings, and fire reviewers can be strict about who stamps them. We put a deferred submittal note on the building set, meaning those drawings follow separately, and we track them until they are approved. Our guide to sprinklers, fire alarms and the fire marshal covers who draws and files each part.

Fire review also reaches into the kitchen layout. On our Prince William County job, the fire marshal required the open space above the walk-in cooler to be boxed out, a detail that touches the cooler, the ceiling and the sprinkler layout at once. Our guide to kitchen hoods, grease and gas explains how those systems are coordinated.

Inspections and opening

Each trade is inspected during construction, and the certificate of occupancy comes last. A building permit alone does not get a restaurant open: the fire, health and occupancy steps still have to close.

In Fairfax County, the certificate of occupancy is the Non-Residential Use Permit, often called a Non-RUP. You apply in PLUS at least five business days before you need it. It is issued once the required reviews are approved and the fees are paid. Depending on the project, those can include the final building inspection, the final site inspection, zoning review and a Health Department inspection. Most sit-down restaurants are assembly uses, and the Fire Marshal’s occupancy page says their pre-occupancy inspection must be requested before applying. That page covers a change in owner or tenant as well as new construction, so a new operator in an existing restaurant should plan for it too. Separately, the Health Department issues the permit to operate after a successful pre-occupancy inspection.

A short Virginia checklist

  1. Confirm the county, city or town from the parcel record.
  2. Ask whether zoning approval comes before the building application or inside it.
  3. Settle the real construction cost and choose the contractor early.
  4. File the health application as soon as the building application is in.
  5. Make sure the sprinkler and hood subcontractors file their fire plans on time.
  6. Answer every county request quickly and keep each online record moving.
  7. In Fairfax County, plan the fire marshal’s pre-occupancy inspection and the Non-RUP at the end.

Questions

Common Questions

Who issues a restaurant building permit in Virginia?

The building department of the county, independent city or town that covers the parcel. Every locality enforces the same Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, but each one runs its own intake, portal and review habits.

Can my architect file the building permit before I pick a contractor?

The statewide code lets the design professional apply, but the contractor's Virginia license must be shown before the permit is issued. In Fairfax County we file as the applicant and add the licensed contractor before issuance, so choosing the contractor early keeps issuance from waiting.

Does the building permit mean my restaurant can open?

No. The fire marshal's review of sprinkler and hood suppression plans, the health department's opening inspection and the final certificate of occupancy are separate steps. Track each of them as closely as the building permit.

What is a Non-RUP in Fairfax County?

A Non-Residential Use Permit, the county's commercial certificate of occupancy. You apply in the county's online system at least five business days before you need it, and it is issued once the required final inspections and reviews are approved and the fees are paid.

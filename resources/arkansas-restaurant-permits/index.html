Opening a Restaurant in Arkansas: Permits and Approvals

Zelta Design · 5 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

In Arkansas, the Arkansas Department of Health reviews a restaurant's food service plans statewide, the city or county building office issues the building permit under the Arkansas Fire Prevention Code, and the local sewer utility usually rules on grease. The order of those steps is set locally: in North Little Rock in 2026, the health letters had to be in hand before the building permit application, so the health packet started first.

In Arkansas, the restaurant approvals are split between the state and the town. The Arkansas Department of Health reviews the food service plans for the whole state. The city or county building office reviews the construction drawings and issues the building permit under a statewide code. The local sewer utility usually has its own say on grease. Additional approvals, such as zoning, signs or a drive-thru, depend on the site and the scope.

The order those steps run in is set locally, and it can change the whole schedule. In North Little Rock in 2026, the city needed the health department’s letters before we could apply for the building permit, so the health packet became the first gate instead of a side track. We are licensed in Arkansas, and this guide separates the statewide rules from what we saw in that one city.

Who reviews what in Arkansas

Three bodies matter on almost every restaurant. Find out early which local office handles each one for your address.

  • Food service: the Arkansas Department of Health. The department runs retail food plan review from its central office in Little Rock. Its plan review packet says the approved plans and letter go to the environmental health specialist at your county health unit, and that a pre-operational inspection, a check before you open, is required.
  • Building and fire: the Arkansas Fire Prevention Code. Arkansas uses one code for fire and building safety statewide, maintained by the State Fire Marshal’s Office. That office reviews state building projects and helps local jurisdictions with plan review when asked, but for a private restaurant the city or county building office is usually the one you file with.
  • Grease: the local sewer utility. The utility that takes the restaurant’s wastewater decides how grease is handled before it reaches the sewer.

For most restaurant work, the drawings must be sealed by an architect licensed in Arkansas. The state’s architecture law has exemptions, so check them before assuming either way. Our guide on whether you need an architect explains how seal rules work.

Step 1: Zoning and the site

Zoning in Arkansas is local. The city’s planning office confirms that a restaurant is allowed on the property and reviews any site, sign or drive-thru changes.

In North Little Rock, the planning department was also the office that handled the building permit on our project, so zoning and building questions went to the same desk. That will not be true everywhere. If the restaurant is in an existing building, ask whether the use is already allowed and whether exterior changes need separate approval. Our guide to restaurant building types explains how a drive-thru changes the review.

Step 2: The health department packet

Start the health packet as soon as the kitchen layout is settled. In some cities the building permit waits for it.

The Department of Health asks for more than drawings. Its packet includes an application, a project cost estimate worksheet with the plan review fee, the menu and the standard operating procedures, the written routines Arkansas food rules require before opening. The packet also says the Food Service Section does not review plumbing plans; those go to the department’s Protective Health Codes office. Plan for two reviews inside one department.

Two habits keep this step moving:

  • Collect the owner’s items first. The construction cost, menu and procedures come from the owner and operator, not the architect. We send that list at the start so the packet is complete when the drawings are.
  • Expect comments. Most packets get at least one round of questions. Our guide to answering plan review comments covers how to close them quickly, and our health department plan review guide covers what the packet contains.

Step 3: Grease and the sewer utility

The local utility, not the health department, usually decides how grease is handled. In North Little Rock in 2026, the city’s wastewater utility reviewed and approved the grease interceptor, a tank that traps grease before the sewer, with its own approval letter ahead of the building permit.

Ask the utility early whether it needs an interceptor, where it may go and what size it expects. The engineering side of grease and kitchen exhaust is covered in our guide to kitchen hoods, grease and gas.

Keep one address on everything

With three or more agencies issuing letters, small differences in the address cause avoidable delays. One letter with a different street suffix or suite number can stop a filing that needs every document to match.

Before anything is filed, we check:

  • the street number, street name and suffix (Street, Avenue, Boulevard) against county parcel or city address records;
  • the suite or unit number, if the space has one;
  • the ZIP code and the city name, which may differ from the mailing city;
  • that the drawings’ title block, every application and the owner’s lease show the same address.

When an approval letter arrives, we compare it with the drawings before attaching it to any other application, and ask the agency to correct it if it differs.

Step 4: The building permit

The city or county building office reviews the sealed construction drawings against the Arkansas Fire Prevention Code and issues the permit. Who may apply, and what they must bring, is a local rule.

In North Little Rock, the general contractor pulls the permit, so the contractor has to be chosen before the permit can be issued. Arkansas licenses commercial contractors through the Arkansas Contractors Licensing Board, and many cities add their own business license or bond requirements. Ask the building office what the contractor must have on file, and run bidding alongside the health review instead of after it. Our permit timeline guide explains how decisions like this set the calendar.

If the store will hold two brands, the agencies still see one restaurant: one set of drawings, one health packet and one grease approval. Our guide to co-branded restaurants covers the design side.

Inspections and opening

Each agency closes out its own approval. The building office inspects the construction and issues the certificate of occupancy after the final inspections. The Department of Health does its pre-operational inspection before it issues the food service permit, and the sewer utility may want to see the installed interceptor. Track them as separate finish lines; our guide to the certificate of occupancy covers the last steps.

A checklist before you sign the lease

  • Ask the city or county which office handles zoning and the building permit, and which approvals it wants before you apply.
  • Settle the kitchen layout early, because the health approval may need to come first.
  • Gather the construction cost estimate, menu and operating procedures for the health packet.
  • Ask the sewer utility how it approves grease interceptors at your address.
  • Confirm one exact address, suite number included, before anything is filed.
  • Choose a contractor who holds the state license and any local license or bond the city requires.

Questions

Common Questions

Who reviews a restaurant's kitchen plans in Arkansas?

The Arkansas Department of Health reviews retail food plans for the whole state from its central office in Little Rock, and sends the approved plans to the county health unit's environmental health specialist. Plumbing plans go to a separate Department of Health office, Protective Health Codes.

Does the health review have to come before the building permit in Arkansas?

There is no single statewide order. In North Little Rock in 2026, the city needed the health department's letters before we could apply for the building permit. Ask the local building office at the start which approvals it wants to see first.

What building code applies to a restaurant in Arkansas?

The Arkansas Fire Prevention Code, which includes the state's building code and is maintained by the State Fire Marshal's Office. Cities and counties issue the permits and inspect, so the local building office is still the one you file with.

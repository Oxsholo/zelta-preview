Opening a Jersey Mike's: Design, Approvals and Permits

Zelta Design · 4 min read · Updated October 11, 2026 · Reviewed by Erik Liepins, RA

The short answer

A Jersey Mike's starts with two brand forms, a site analysis of the space and a design request that sets the seats, grill, ordering points and ceiling. The brand then issues a layout, and the architect's construction drawings are reviewed against it sheet by sheet before they go to the landlord and the building department.

A Jersey Mike’s is usually a storefront or inline unit, and the design process is driven by the brand’s own forms. Two of them come first: a site analysis that describes the space as it is, and a design request where the franchisee makes the choices that shape the layout. The brand then issues a layout, and the architect’s construction drawings must match it before they go to the landlord and the building department.

We have designed Jersey Mike’s stores in New York City and New Jersey, including our Jersey Mike’s in East Midtown, a storefront with glass, wood finishes and light blue tile. This guide follows the order the work actually ran in. For investment figures, see Item 7 of the brand’s Franchise Disclosure Document.

Step 1: The site analysis

Before any layout, the brand wants the existing space documented. Its site analysis report asks for the things that decide whether the standard layout fits:

  • the ceiling height and the height to the underside of the structure and the lowest beam;
  • whether the space is sprinklered, and where the fire alarm panel is, with photos;
  • the construction of the exterior walls, the demising walls between tenants and their fire rating;
  • the floor and roof construction, and the neighboring tenants.

For the East Midtown store, the report recorded the ceiling and beam heights and the fire alarm panel’s location in the basement. Measured information like this comes from an existing conditions survey, and it is worth doing before the lease is final.

Step 2: The design request

The design request turns the franchisee’s choices into the layout. On the brand’s form we have worked from, the questions included:

  • the number of seats, and whether there are TVs in the dining room;
  • one register or two, with wiring for a future second register as standard;
  • whether to keep any existing equipment or existing restrooms;
  • the grill size, where a larger grill also brings extra cold storage at the hot sub station;
  • the pickup tower size, which affects what can be wired for later;
  • whether two lines face customers, which needs peak volume documentation and sign-off;
  • whether a second entrance is needed, for exiting, parking or a rear sidewalk;
  • the dining room ceiling: a drop ceiling at the existing height, a raised one, or open.

The form also noted the brand’s preference for a single accessible restroom where the code allows it. Whether it does depends on the occupant load and the local plumbing code; see our guide to restaurant restrooms and fixtures.

Step 3: The approved layout and brand review

The brand’s layout becomes the reference for everything after it. Our construction drawings are reviewed against that approved layout, sheet by sheet.

On the East Midtown store, the brand’s design team commented on the architectural, electrical, plumbing and sprinkler sheets. The comments covered:

  • Finishes by zone. Quarry tile under the whole front line and into the walk-in cooler; wall tile in the kitchen only as far back as customers can see; a set wall finish at both hoods.
  • Equipment. The grill size and its hood, numbered equipment tags matching the schedule, and the base cabinets under the drink dispenser.
  • Lighting. Track lights for the menu board and the meat case, and the brand’s light fixture suppliers listed on the drawings.
  • Power and data. Both electrical panels side by side, printers at the hot sub area and second line, a future second register, and connections placed where equipment can plug straight in.

The more of this the first set gets right, the fewer rounds the review takes. Our guide to franchise brand approvals covers how these reviews work across brands.

Who furnishes what

A Jersey Mike’s build-out has three or more buyers, and a responsibility matrix keeps them straight. On the stores we designed, the matrix split it this way:

  • The franchisee furnished, the contractor installed: dining furniture, graphics and the menu board, the point of sale system, the kitchen exhaust hood, its fire suppression system and the make-up air unit.
  • The contractor furnished and installed: casework and millwork, the water heater, the mop sink, restroom fixtures and grab bars, and the complete sprinkler system.
  • The beverage supplier furnished and installed: the fountain dispenser and syrup lines, with the contractor providing power and drains.

Put the matrix in the bid documents so no item falls between them. See kitchen equipment and furniture.

The landlord’s review

In a multi-tenant building, the landlord reviews the drawings before the permit. In a Manhattan office building, the landlord’s engineer for our East Midtown store asked for demolition on overtime hours, work coordinated with the property manager, a kitchen make-up air intake independent of the building’s existing unit, a fire and smoke damper on the fresh air intake, and an odor control unit on the exhaust. Our guide to getting kitchen exhaust out of a city storefront covers the exhaust side.

Permits, fire and opening

The building department reviews the same drawings once the brand and landlord are satisfied. In New York City, the East Midtown store’s general construction, mechanical, plumbing and sprinkler work was approved by the Department of Buildings, and before opening the Fire Department’s range hood inspection unit inspected the hood’s wet chemical fire suppression system and issued its letter of approval. Our New York City permits guide covers that process.

Questions

Common Questions

What does Jersey Mike's need before the layout is drawn?

On the stores we have designed, the brand used a site analysis report describing the existing space and a design request form where the franchisee chose the seat count, grill size, number of registers, pickup tower size and ceiling type.

Does Jersey Mike's review the architect's drawings?

Yes. On our East Midtown store, the brand's design team reviewed the construction drawings sheet by sheet against the approved layout, down to finish tags, equipment numbers, light fixture suppliers and printer locations.

Who buys the equipment and furniture for a Jersey Mike's?

A responsibility matrix splits it. On the stores we designed, the franchisee furnished most equipment, seating, graphics and the kitchen hood for the contractor to install, the contractor furnished casework and fire protection, and the beverage supplier furnished the fountain system.

How much does it cost to open a Jersey Mike's?

Item 7 of the brand's Franchise Disclosure Document gives the brand's own estimate of the initial investment. The build-out part depends on the space, the hood and exhaust route and what the landlord requires.
